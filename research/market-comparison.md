## Ranking

1. **EU (Croatia):** cheapest real-money launch, lowest break-even among the big markets, closest, and Croatia's e-invoicing gives a strong anti-fraud check.
2. **UAE:** a licence that fits PayDay almost exactly and a registry that blocks double financing, but about 3× the break-even volume and someone must live there.
3. **USA:** the biggest prize and the hardest, most expensive start. Don't skip it; enter in two steps:
   - first, US accredited investors fund EU invoices (no US SME licensing needed);
   - later, PayDay as software for a US factoring company in a light-touch state such as Texas.
4. **North Macedonia:** last for real money (no crypto law, €500k licence, small market, break-even bigger than the market), but **first for phase 2**: test money needs no licence, and you can sit down with Prilep SMEs and their clients in person.
   - **Upgrade trigger:** if 2 of these 3 happen, move it up to #2 for real money: (1) the e-Invoicing Law is adopted and mandatory from 1 Apr 2027; (2) a MiCA-style crypto law with a stablecoin regime is adopted; (3) a licensed Macedonian financial company signs a pilot agreement.
   - **Revisit:** mid-2027.

## Changes that help in all four markets

1. **No admin power over tokens.** Remove any power to move, burn or reassign an invoice token, and let the emergency pause only stop new listings and trades. In the US this is what makes the token holder the legal owner under Article 12; elsewhere it makes partners and regulators more comfortable. Cheap to do now, before the audit.
2. **One official invoice ID = one token.** Croatia (Fiskalizacija 2.0) and North Macedonia (e-Faktura) both give each invoice a tax-office ID. Hash the e-invoice XML on-chain and reject duplicates. Build it once, use it in both.
3. **Client confirmation = legal notice of assignment.** In Croatia and North Macedonia the buyer the debtor was notified about first wins a double-sale fight, not the token. Get a lawyer to word the confirm-by-email step so it counts as notice.
4. **Hashes only on-chain.** Names and amounts stay off-chain (GDPR in the EU, the GDPR-aligned law in North Macedonia).
5. **Plan for recourse.** Licensed partners in every market will likely want recourse to the SME or a first-loss reserve, not today's "investor takes the loss" rule.
